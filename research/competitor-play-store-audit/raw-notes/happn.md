## Full description (verbatim, for internal analysis only)
Opens with a compact four-word verb chain ("Like - Crush - Chat - Date") as a functional tagline,
then leads the body copy with a large global social-proof number (140 million users). Structured with
bolded mini section-headers acting as pseudo-bullets ("Date and meet people," "Crush with peace of
mind...", "...with relevant profiles, while having fun!", "Make it happn") each followed by a short
paragraph — a magazine-style narrative format rather than a plain feature bullet list. Core hook is
the "geo-proximity/crossing paths" mechanic (real-life places you've been, not blind swiping), with
safety/privacy reassurance given its own dedicated section (invisible location, only "crossing points"
shown, no unsolicited messages). Introduces named proprietary features as capitalized brand terms
(Teasers, Hobbies, CrushTime, SuperCrush, Crush) that double as glossary/keyword building. Mentions
Premium subscription benefits (see who already liked you, more SuperCrushes) near the end, with no
emoji at all and no explicit pricing/legal auto-renewal disclosure in the visible text (lighter legal
footprint than QuackQuack or Tinder). Closes with a wordplay callback to the brand name ("there is
only one place where it's really happn-ing!").

## What's new

## Notable ASO/attractiveness components observed
- Title keeps the brand name minimal ("happn: dating app") and relies on category badge + reviews for credibility rather than keyword-stacked subtitle.
- Category rank badge ("#8 top free dating") is weaker positioning than higher-ranked competitors but still lends Google-granted social proof.
- Differentiated core hook (real-life proximity/"crossing paths") is reinforced consistently in both description section headers and a dedicated screenshot (the map UI), giving the listing a clear unique-mechanic narrative distinct from generic swipe apps.
- Proprietary named features (Crush, SuperCrush, CrushTime, Teasers, Hobbies) are used consistently across description and screenshots, functioning as ownable brand vocabulary/keywords.
- Explicitly broadens beyond romance with a "Friendship / Hangout" intent screenshot, mirroring how QuackQuack and Tinder both also market a friendship use case.
- Elegant serif display typography on dark backgrounds gives a more premium/editorial visual tone versus the brighter, more literal UI-screenshot approach of QuackQuack — a brand-forward vs. feature-forward contrast worth noting for Riteangle's own tone decision.
- Reviews surfaced on the page skew negative around premium paywall frustration and forced Google Sign-In — a reputation risk pattern to watch for regardless of listing polish.
